Answer: 144

Eilenberg--Mac Lane objects in an \(\infty\)-topos represent sheaf
cohomology. Therefore
\[
\pi_0
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal X}
(1_{\mathcal X},K(\mathcal A,1))
\cong
H^1(T^2;\mathcal A).
\]

Because
\[
T^2\simeq B\mathbb Z^2
\]
and \(\mathcal A\) is the local system corresponding to the
\(\mathbb Z^2\)-module \(A=(\mathbb Z/12)^2\), this is group cohomology:
\[
H^1(T^2;\mathcal A)
\cong
H^1(\mathbb Z^2;A).
\]

The standard Koszul cochain complex computing the cohomology of
\(\mathbb Z^2\) is
\[
A\xrightarrow{d^0}A\oplus A\xrightarrow{d^1}A,
\]
with
\[
d^0(a)=((M-I)a,(N-I)a),
\]
and
\[
d^1(u,v)=(N-I)u-(M-I)v.
\]

Writing
\[
u=(u_1,u_2),\qquad v=(v_1,v_2),
\]
we have
\[
(M-I)(x,y)=(2y,0),
\qquad
(N-I)(x,y)=(3y,0).
\]
Thus
\[
d^1(u,v)=(3u_2-2v_2,0).
\]

The homomorphism
\[
(\mathbb Z/12)^2\to\mathbb Z/12,
\qquad
(u_2,v_2)\mapsto3u_2-2v_2
\]
is surjective because
\[
\gcd(12,3,2)=1.
\]
Its kernel therefore has \(12\) elements. The coordinates \(u_1,v_1\)
are arbitrary, giving
\[
|\ker d^1|=12\cdot12^2=1728.
\]

Meanwhile
\[
d^0(x,y)=((2y,0),(3y,0)).
\]
The map \(y\mapsto(2y,3y)\) is injective modulo \(12\), so
\[
|\operatorname{im}d^0|=12.
\]

Therefore
\[
|H^1(\mathbb Z^2;A)|
=
\frac{1728}{12}
=
144.
